Recommended Path · Natural Language Processing

Text & Natural Language Data Annotation

Learn how unstructured text is labeled, structured, and turned into precise training datasets for Large Language Models (LLMs) and NLP pipelines.

Before virtual assistants can understand user intent or an LLM can generate accurate summaries, humans must structure human language into labeled training signals.

Text annotation converts raw documents, customer logs, and conversations into machine-readable format. Below is your detailed breakdown of the four core natural language labeling techniques.

✓ Named Entity Recognition (NER) ✓ Sentiment & Intent Classification ✓ Relationship Extraction ✓ Text Summarization & RLHF

01 · Information Extraction

Named Entity Recognition (NER)

NER identifies key entities within unstructured text strings and categorizes them into predefined classes like names, dates, organizations, locations, or monetary figures.

Standard Entities

Highlighting universal entities like Person, Date, Location, and Organization within body text.

Domain-Specific Entities

Tagging specialized terminology such as medical drug names, clinical diagnoses, legal statute codes, or financial ticker symbols.

Example & Text Logic: In medical records processing, an annotator highlights text like Aspirin 81mg and tags it as [MEDICATION_DOSAGE].

02 · Categorical & Intent Tagging

Sentiment & Intent Classification

Document-level and sentence-level classification assigns qualitative labels to evaluate tone, intent, topic clusters, or safety flags across customer text.

Sentiment Analysis

Evaluating subjective tone within reviews or tweets into classes like Positive, Neutral, Negative, or Frustrated.

Intent Classification

Categorizing user support queries by goal (e.g., mapping "Where is my box?" to the intent [TRACK_ORDER]).

Example: E-commerce automated support routes incoming messages automatically based on intent tags assigned during training data labeling.

03 · Knowledge Mapping

Relationship Extraction & Syntax Parsing

Relationship extraction connects distinct tagged entities within a document to define how they relate to one another, building structured knowledge graphs.

  • Semantic Relationships: Link entities through contextual associations (e.g., linking "Tim Cook" to "Apple" via the relation [CEO_OF]).
  • Coreference Resolution: Connects pronouns to their proper original nouns (e.g., linking "she" in sentence 2 back to "Dr. Sarah" in sentence 1).
  • Dependency Parsing: Tagging sentence structure (subject, verb, object) to aid linguistic translation and parsing engines.

Example: Legal tech tools use relationship mapping to identify contractual obligations between Party A and Party B across hundreds of contract pages.

04 · Generative AI & Safety

RLHF, Prompt Alignment & Summarization

Modern generative AI requires human feedback loop data (RLHF) to align language model outputs for safety, helpfulness, and factual accuracy.

Response Ranking & Preference

Comparing multiple AI outputs for the same prompt and ranking them based on clarity, truthfulness, and safety compliance.

Red Teaming & Safety Tagging

Identifying toxic text, hallucinations, bias, or sensitive personal identifiable information (PII) for redaction or filtering.

Example: AI evaluators review generated code or summaries, flagging subtle factual hallucinations before model deployment.
