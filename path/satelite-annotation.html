Geographic & Satellite Data Annotation

Learn how Earth observation imagery, aerial drone photos, and GIS spatial vector data are mapped, segmented, and labeled for environmental and urban AI models.

Analyzing planet-scale imagery requires specialized spatial annotation techniques. Unlike standard RGB images, satellite and aerial datasets feature non-standard resolutions, geographic coordinate systems, and multi-spectral light bands.

Geospatial annotation converts raw remote sensing data into structured GeoJSON vectors, land cover maps, and infrastructure layers. Below is your detailed breakdown of four core satellite labeling methodologies.

✓ Vector Mapping (Polygons & Polylines) ✓ Land Use / Land Cover (LULC) ✓ Multispectral & SAR Analysis ✓ Change Detection & Time-Series

01 · Spatial Feature Extraction

GIS Vector Mapping (Polygons, Lines & Points)

Vector mapping extracts geometric features directly from aerial photos, binding pixel regions to real-world coordinates (latitude, longitude) for Geographic Information Systems (GIS).

Polylines & Road Networks

Tracing line vectors along road centrelines, railway tracks, shipping corridors, and utility pipelines to build navigation graphs.

Building Footprints & Polygons

Delineating precise structural roof outlines and agricultural plot boundaries using multi-vertex GIS polygons.

Example & Spatial Logic: Urban planning AI uses automated building footprint segmentation to monitor municipal expansion and tax assessment zones.

02 · Environmental Mapping

Land Use & Land Cover (LULC) Segmentation

LULC classification assigns every pixel in an Earth observation scene to specific terrain categories, enabling macro-environmental monitoring across vast territories.

Natural Resource Classes

Segmenting ecological zones into classes such as Dense Forest, Water Bodies, Wetlands, or Bare Soil.

Anthropogenic Classes

Tagging human-influenced zones including High-Density Residential, Industrial Parks, and Crop Fields.

Example: Climate organizations train segmentation algorithms on LULC data to track global deforestation rates and urban heat islands.

03 · Advanced Sensors

Multispectral, Hyperspectral & Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)

Satellite imagery often extends beyond visible light into Near-Infrared (NIR), Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR), and Radar channels to reveal hidden ground features.

  • NDVI & Vegetation Indexing: Annotating vegetation health and crop yield indicators using Near-Infrared band composite images.
  • SAR Radar Interpretation: Labeling Synthetic Aperture Radar images that penetrate cloud cover and operate during night passes.
  • Thermal & Mineral Mapping: Annotating thermal signatures to locate ocean heat anomalies or ground mineral compositions.

Example: Precision agriculture AI combines NIR band annotations with drone imagery to identify crop disease weeks before it becomes visible to the human eye.

04 · Temporal Earth Observation

Geospatial Change Detection & Time-Series

Change detection compares aligned satellite images of the exact same geographical coordinate captured over different dates (`Time A` vs `Time B`) to flag physical shifts.

Disaster Assessment

Highlighting structural damage, flooded regions, or wildfire burn scars immediately following natural disasters.

Infrastructure Growth

Tracking long-term construction progress, new road development, or port container volume changes over multi-year timelines.

Example: Emergency response agencies process post-disaster satellite imagery within hours to direct rescue teams toward damaged roads and bridges.
